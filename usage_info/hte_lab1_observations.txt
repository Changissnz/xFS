<HTESurface> uses only undirected simple graphs. Some graphs will prove to 
be relatively challenging to an <HTENavigator>. These graphs will be of relatively 
large size, more than a thousand nodes, with a low connectivity ratio. For one, a 
<HTENavigator> will not have as many options per node location to steer away from 
probable threat nodes. Because of this quality, an <HTENavigator> with knowledge of 
prior <HTENavigator> performance will have non-empty sets of nodes for these 
variables: `take`,`possible_avoid`, and `avoid`. Large set sizes for the variables 
`possible_avoid` and `avoid` result in lesser options for an <HTENavigator> moving 
to the next node. The <HTENavigator> will prioritize next candidates that it have 
already traveled, sometimes resulting in circular travel patterns that make no 
real progress in the navigator reaching an objective node. In these cases of 
circular decision-making due to constraints imposed by `possible_avoid` and `avoid` 
variables, <HTENavigator> will terminate in navigation due to its fuel reaching 0 
(a loss for the navigator). In this first lab concerning the problem of Hidden 
Threat Exposure on a relatively connected graph, the issue of circular travel from 
programmed cautioning is less present.

The design of <SimulationSolutionSearch> for <HTEBot> considers 40 mode configurations 
of one <HTEBot>. See file<hte_ext> for specific programming. In short, these are the 
variables (the bot mode) for 
- navigator_remembers_past_encounters (size 2) -> boolean, quality of <HTENavigator>
- navigator_uses_isomorphic_prediction (size 2) -> boolean, quality of <HTENavigator>
- memory_less navigator (size 2) -> boolean, quality of <HTENavigator>
- contra_risk (size 5) -> value in [0.,1.], quality of <HTENavigator>. 

In this sequence of trials, <HTENavigator> does not have much information to base 
better navigation decisions on. These are the number of navigators used for every 
bot mode configuration. 
- In Trial 1, there are 10 navigators before the <HTESurface> switches to another 
isomorphism. And then there are 10 navigators after that. 
- In Trial 2, there are 30 total navigators, ten for the original, ten for the 1st 
isomorphic surface, ten for the 2nd isomorphic surface. 
- In Trial 3, there are 40 total navigators, ten for the original, and ten for every 
successive isomorphic surface after. 

In technical terms, more navigators would reveal the locations of more threats, 
up until all threats have been encountered once. If the lifespan of a threat node 
is finite at integer x, the minimum number of navigators required to remove the threat 
is x. Since there are less than 50 navigators (40 to be precise) used for any bot mode 
configuration in this lab, the most number of threat nodes known by previous encounter 
is 39 (19 for Trial 1, 29 for Trial 2, and 39 for Trial 3).  

These trials attempt to find the best solution, comprised of a pair, <HTEBot> bot 
mode and a pseudo-random number generator(PRNG). The PRNG is used by the <HTENavigator> 
to make navigation decisions. 

These three trials are certainly not conclusive on what bot mode yields the greatest 
result. The objective of these trials is to determine the best bot mode for a navigator 
to have the greatest improvements in performance between every isomorphic surface 
in a sequence of <HTESurface> instances. As clarification, this objective 
(see function<HTE_env_cmp_function_type_2_>) is different than merely focusing on 
navigators' overall performance, their path lengths and success rate (see 
function<HTE_env_cmp_function_type_1_>).

from graph_models.graph_gen import * 
from graph_problems.hte_ext import * 
from graph_problems.sim_solution_search import * 
from morebs2.numerical_generator import prg_to_prg__LCG_sequence
import time 

#### generate the <HTESurface> 
num_entry_points = 20
num_objective_points = 15
threat_ratio = 0.1
threat_mobility_ratio = 0.75  
threat_nodes_include_entry_points = False  

D = generated_graph_sample_1000(1200,0.01)
prg = prg__LCG(55.6,63.44,-1174.1174,19199.5) 
htes = HTESurface.generate_instance(D,num_entry_points,num_objective_points,\
    threat_ratio,threat_mobility_ratio,threat_nodes_include_entry_points,\
    prg)

#### generate <HTEBot> 
hteb = HTEBot(htes,None,navigator_remembers_past_encounters=False,verbose=True)
hteb.set_bot_mode(np.array([0,0,0,0]))

num_navigators = 10 
num_iso_surfaces = 1
run_function = HTE_env_run_(num_navigators,num_iso_surfaces)

prg_seq = prg_to_prg__LCG_sequence(prg,1) 
assignment_func = HTE_env_prng_assignment_function

mode_shifter = HTEBotModeShifter().shift_HTEBot 
solution_fetcher = HTE_env_solution_fetch_function

success_multiplier = -100.0 
function_type = 2 
cmp_solution_function = HTE_env_cmp_solution(function_type,\
    num_isos=num_iso_surfaces,success_multiplier= success_multiplier)

#### start up the solution search
S = SimulationSolutionSearch(hteb,run_function,prg_seq,assignment_func,\
    mode_shifter,solution_fetcher,cmp_solution_function,verbose=True)

t = time.time() 
while not S.fin_stat: 
    t2 = time.time() 
    S.process_one()
    print("-- timenga: ",time.time() - t2)

print("total runtime: {} seconds.".format(time.time() - t))
