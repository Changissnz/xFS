Out of the 9 trials with ending results displayed in file `sb_lab1.txt`, trials 
1,2,4,5, and 6, where <StrangleForm> cannot consume nodes it currently holds, 
<StrangleForm> loses, unable to completely capture the intact graph G after 
exhausting its energy stores. Specifically, <StrangleForm> in trial 1 reached 
past 600 nodes in strangleholds, out of a total of 634, at timestamp 50. For the 
remaining timestamps, up until <StrangleForm>'s termination, <StrangleForm> could 
not reach total stranglehold of the graph. <StrangleForm> oscillates in the 
approximate range of 580-620 for the remainder of the simulation. 

LEMMA: If a <StrangleForm> cannot consume the nodes it holds, there is an 
unspecified probability that the <StrangleForm> will plateau in the maximum 
number of nodes it holds. After reaching this plateau, <StrangleForm>'s number 
of held nodes will oscillate in a range that does not include the total number 
of nodes of graph. This is due to three primary features. The first feature is 
the parameter of the range DEFAULT_STRANGLE_ENV_ENTRY_POINTS that sets the 
limit on the number of new nodes for a <StrangleForm> to hold with a new 
strangling body. The second feature is the DFS process used by every active 
strangling body of the <StrangleForm>. If a node that has already been held 
through a previous DFS process P0 is touched by another DFS process P1, process 
P1 terminates. This technicality means that many DFS processes will be prematurely 
terminated before they have a chance to capture nodes that are not held. The 
last feature is of the <StrangleSubject>. After a certain ratio of nodes are 
put into strangleholds by the <StrangleForm>, this being the plateau, there is 
an almost 100% probability that the <StrangleSubject> will decide on a 
(community nodeset of graph, total breaking force) that frees up that nodeset of 
the graph, due to the programmed functions that <StrangleSubject> uses to make 
its decision in information modes 0-3. So a <StrangleForm> will not be able to 
exceed this plateau, as the <StrangleSubject> frees a ratio of the strangled 
nodes at one timestamp that roughly matches the number of nodes the <StrangleForm> 
can put into strangleholds at the next timestamp.
